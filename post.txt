🌙 WITCHES’ SABBATH (1798) — FRANCISCO GOYA’S DARK VISION OF THE SUPERNATURAL 🎨

What happens when fear, superstition, and imagination come together in a single painting?

Created by Spanish artist Francisco Goya between 1797 and 1798, Witches’ Sabbath (El aquelarre) is a haunting oil painting that draws viewers into a mysterious world of witchcraft and supernatural imagery.

🖼️ WHAT DOES THE PAINTING SHOW?

At the center stands a large, dark, horned goat-like figure, surrounded by a group of people gathered in an isolated landscape beneath a night sky. Some figures appear to watch the central creature, while others sit or kneel around it.

A crescent moon hangs in the upper-left corner, and dark, bat-like shapes move across the sky, adding to the painting’s unsettling atmosphere.

The horned figure is commonly associated with the Devil in traditional European depictions of witches’ gatherings. The scene evokes a witches’ sabbath—a gathering traditionally imagined in folklore as a meeting between witches and supernatural forces.

Goya uses dramatic contrasts between darkness and light, expressive faces, and unusual figures to create a scene that feels mysterious, disturbing, and dreamlike.

🔍 WHO CREATED IT, AND WHY IS IT IMPORTANT?

Francisco Goya (1746–1828) was one of Spain’s most influential artists. His work explored a wide range of subjects, including human behavior, social criticism, superstition, violence, and the darker side of the imagination.

This painting belongs to a group of works associated with witchcraft that Goya produced during the late eighteenth century. Such imagery can be understood within the historical context of European beliefs about witchcraft and the fascination with the supernatural.

Although the painting presents a supernatural scene, it should be understood as an artistic depiction of witchcraft imagery—not as evidence that the events shown actually occurred.

📜 THE HISTORY BEHIND THE CANVAS

In 1798, the painting was purchased alongside five other works related to witchcraft by the Duke and Duchess of Osuna. It is not known for certain whether the couple commissioned these paintings or purchased them after Goya had completed them.

During the twentieth century, the painting was acquired by Spanish financier and art collector José Lázaro Galdiano. Following his death, it was donated to the Spanish state.

Today, the painting is held at the Museo Lázaro Galdiano in Madrid, Spain.

📌 ARTWORK DETAILS

🎨 Title: Witches’ Sabbath (El aquelarre)
👨‍🎨 Artist: Francisco Goya
📅 Date: 1797–1798
🖌️ Medium: Oil on canvas
📐 Dimensions: 43 × 30 cm (approximately 17 × 12 inches)
🏛️ Current location: Museo Lázaro Galdiano, Madrid, Spain

💭 WHY DOES THIS PAINTING STILL CAPTURE OUR ATTENTION?

Perhaps its greatest power lies in what it leaves unexplained. The figures, the horned creature, the moonlit sky, and the surrounding darkness invite us to consider how fear and superstition shaped the human imagination.

Goya does not present a comforting fantasy. Instead, he creates an unsettling visual world that encourages us to examine the relationship between belief, fear, and the unknown.

Nearly 230 years later, the painting remains a fascinating example of how art can transform the supernatural into something vivid, mysterious, and unforgettable.

Sometimes, a painting does more than show us a scene—it reveals the fears and beliefs of the world that imagined it. 🌑
